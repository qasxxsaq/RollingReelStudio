Clara loved her quiet apartment. Every evening, she would sit by the window with a cup of tea, watching the city lights flicker.

One night, there was a knock at her door. She wasn’t expecting anyone. When she opened it, a man stood there in a gray coat, holding a notebook.

“May I come in?” he asked. His voice was calm, almost too calm.

Against her instinct, she stepped aside. The man walked in, opened the notebook, and smiled. “I’ve been following you,” he said. “Or rather… someone has. And now I’m here to help.”

Clara frowned. “I don’t understand.”

He flipped the notebook open. The pages were filled with every detail of her life: her morning coffee, the song she hummed, even the dream she had last night. “You see,” he said softly, “you’re not supposed to exist yet. You’re… early.”

“What do you mean?” she whispered.

Before he could answer, the lights flickered. Clara blinked—and suddenly, she was staring at the notebook alone. The man had vanished.

On the first page, a new line had appeared, written in her handwriting:
"I remember him. But I’ve never met him… yet."

As she closed the notebook, a chilling realization hit her: she had been living someone else’s memory all along—and maybe, she wasn’t the one in control.
